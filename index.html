Emotional regulation pocket guide

What do I need right now?

A quick, practical guide to choosing and remembering common DBT skills for emotional regulation. Tap the situation that best fits what is happening, then open a skill for a concise reminder.

How to use it

You do not need to work through every skill. Start with the problem in front of you. If emotion is too intense to think clearly, regulate first. If another person is involved, decide whether your priority is the objective, the relationship or your self-respect.

Save it like an app

iPhone: open this page in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Android: use your browser menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen. Once loaded, the guide is designed to work offline.

A useful order when emotions are high
STOP → TIP/TIPP if needed → Check the Facts → Pros & Cons / Wise Mind → interpersonal skill if another person is involved.
My emotion is overwhelmingSTOP, TIP/TIPP
Distress tolerance
STOP
Before acting impulsively when emotion is high.
S – Stop. Do not immediately act on the urge.
T – Take a step back. Create some space.
O – Observe. Notice thoughts, feelings, body sensations, urges and facts.
P – Proceed mindfully. Choose what is effective rather than what merely relieves the emotion now.
Work example: draft the angry email, but do not send it yet.
Distress tolerance
TIP / TIPP
When arousal is so high that clear thinking is difficult.
T – Temperature. Cool the face safely with cold water or a cold pack.
I – Intense exercise. Brief vigorous movement, where medically appropriate.
P – Paced breathing. Slow breathing, often with a longer out-breath.
P – Paired/progressive muscle relaxation. Tense and release muscle groups with the breath.
Cold exposure and intense exercise are not appropriate for everyone. Adapt for relevant medical conditions.
I have a strong urge to actPros & Cons, Opposite Action
Distress tolerance
Pros & Cons
When deciding whether to act on a strong urge.
Pros of acting on the urge – including immediate relief.
Cons of acting on the urge – including later consequences.
Pros of resisting the urge – what becomes possible if you wait or choose differently.
Cons of resisting the urge – including discomfort or a sense of injustice.

Consider consequences now, later today, tomorrow and in the longer term.

Emotion regulation
Opposite Action
When acting on the emotion's urge would be ineffective.

Identify the emotion's action urge, then deliberately do the effective opposite.

For excessive anger, that may mean lowering your voice, unclenching, approaching calmly and trying to understand, while still keeping legitimate boundaries.
I need to make a decisionWise Mind, Check the Facts
Mindfulness & decisions
Wise Mind
When emotion and logic pull in different directions.

Emotion Mind is dominated by feeling and urges. Reasonable Mind is dominated by facts and logic. Wise Mind integrates both.

Ask: What are the facts? What is my emotion telling me? What matters to me? What action is most effective in the longer term?
Emotion regulation
Check the Facts
When emotion may be amplified by assumptions or interpretations.

Separate observable facts from interpretation. Identify the emotion and ask whether its intensity fits the facts and the actual threat.

“She spoke to management without telling us” may be a fact. “She wanted to destroy the team” is an interpretation unless there is evidence for motive.
I need to ask, say no or address conflictDEAR MAN
Interpersonal effectiveness – objective
DEAR MAN
For requests, boundaries, saying no and addressing problems.
D – Describe. State the relevant facts without judgement.
E – Express. Say how the situation affects you.
A – Assert. Clearly ask for what you want or say no.
R – Reinforce. Explain the benefit of the requested change.
M – Mindful. Stay on the point.
A – Appear confident. Calm voice and clear wording.
N – Negotiate. Consider workable alternatives.
First ask: “What concrete outcome do I want from this conversation?”
I want to protect the relationshipGIVE
Interpersonal effectiveness – relationship
GIVE
G – Gentle. Avoid attacks, threats and contempt.
I – Interested. Listen.
V – Validate. Acknowledge what is understandable without pretending to agree.
E – Easy manner. Keep the interaction as calm as circumstances allow.
GIVE does not mean trusting, liking or agreeing with someone.
I want to protect my self-respectFAST
Interpersonal effectiveness – self-respect
FAST
F – Fair. Be fair to yourself and the other person.
A – no unnecessary Apologies.
S – Stick to your values.
T – Truthful. Do not exaggerate or invent motives.
Ask: “When I look back on this in six months, how would I want to have behaved?”

I am fighting realityRadical Acceptance, Turning the Mind
Reality acceptance
Radical Acceptance

Acceptance means acknowledging reality as it is, not approving of it. You can accept that something happened and still decide it was wrong or that action is needed.

Turning the Mind: notice that you have gone back to fighting reality and choose acceptance again.
